# Market

**Demo content.** Every company named here is fictional and invented for this
template. Do not copy a competitive framing of real companies into a public
repository.

## Who we sell to

Mid-market B2B software and services companies, 50 to 500 employees, with a
sales team between 5 and 40 people and a CRM already in place. The buyer is
usually a VP Sales or a Head of Growth. RevOps is almost always in the room and
is often the person who decides.

## What they are trying to fix

- Reps spend their day on follow-up rather than conversations.
- Reply volume outgrew the team, and the good replies are getting lost in it.
- Sending domains have been flagged at least once, and nobody is sure why.
- They bought something similar before and it stalled after a quarter.

That last one shapes every deal. Most of our prospects are not new to this
category, they are disappointed by it.

## Competitive landscape (fictional)

| Category | Who | How we are positioned against them |
|---|---|---|
| Full-stack AI sellers | Relaypoint, Sendwright | They sell autonomy. We sell what happens after the send. Where a buyer wants a machine that runs itself, they are the better fit and we should say so. |
| Data and orchestration | Cadence Nine | Adjacent rather than competing; several customers run both. |
| Classic sequencers | Prospectly | Cheaper and more established. We lose on price and win on reply handling and deliverability. |

Describe a competitor by what their product does. Never characterise them. See
`.claude/skills/acme-marketing/references/claims.md` for what may be said in
public.

## Where we win and lose

**Win** when the buyer already has volume and their problem is quality, when
RevOps is involved, and when a previous tool disappointed them.

**Lose** when the buyer wants the cheapest sequencer, when there is no existing
sales motion to scale, or when procurement needs a long enterprise cycle that
our short implementation does not reassure them about.
