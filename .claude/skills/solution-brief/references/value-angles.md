# Value angles

Pick exactly one. The angle determines the structure of the whole brief, which
is why it is chosen before drafting rather than during.

Replace these with your own during install. What transfers is the discipline:
a short, closed list, each with the signal that selects it, and a note on when
it is the wrong choice.

## 1. Time to first result

**Choose when** the buyer has tried something similar and it stalled, or the
team is small and the cost of a long implementation is the real objection.

**Argument** - they are running within a day, and the first measurable outcome
lands in the first week.

**Wrong choice when** the buyer is large and procurement-led; speed reads as
"not enterprise ready".

## 2. Quality of what comes back

**Choose when** the buyer already has volume and their problem is that the
output is noisy: wrong replies, wasted follow-up, a team that has stopped
trusting the tool.

**Argument** - the difference is in what happens after the send, not the send.

**Wrong choice when** they have no volume yet; there is nothing to improve.

## 3. Risk and control

**Choose when** the buyer is in a regulated or reputation-sensitive business, or
when procurement, security or legal is visibly in the room.

**Argument** - guardrails, limits and the ability to stop something before it
becomes a problem.

**Wrong choice when** the buyer is a founder-led team optimising for speed; it
reads as friction.

## Choosing

Read the discovery calls and find what the buyer said twice. People repeat what
actually worries them. If nothing repeats, discovery is not finished: say so
rather than picking the angle that is easiest to write.
